# CS-250-Sprint-Review-and-Retrospective

Course Reflection

Throughout CS 250, I gained a better understanding of Agile development and how the different Scrum roles work together to deliver software that meets customer needs. By working as the Scrum Master, Product Owner, tester, and developer, I was able to see how communication, planning, and flexibility contribute to a successful project.

How do I interpret user needs and implement them into a program? How does creating user stories help with this?

Understanding user needs starts with listening to what the customer wants and determining how those requests can become working features. During the SNHU Travel project, I learned how to take customer feedback and turn it into user stories that explain what the user needs and why. User stories help the development team understand the purpose behind each feature while providing a starting point for planning and testing. Working as a tester also showed me the importance of clear acceptance criteria so the team can verify that the finished product meets the customer's expectations.

How do I approach developing programs? What Agile processes do I hope to incorporate into my future development work?

When developing programs, I prefer to break larger tasks into smaller, manageable pieces rather than trying to complete everything at once. Throughout this course, I learned how Agile supports this approach through Sprint Planning, Daily Scrums, and regular feedback. One example was updating the travel slideshow application when the Product Owner introduced new requirements. This experience showed me that changes are a normal part of software development and that being flexible is important. Moving forward, I would like to incorporate regular communication, incremental development, and retrospectives into my work to identify problems early and continuously improve.

What does it mean to be a good team member in software development?

Being a good team member means communicating clearly, taking responsibility for your work, and being willing to help others when needed. Our Vision Quest Software discussion reinforced how important it is for the different Scrum roles to work together instead of focusing only on their individual responsibilities. I also learned that asking questions and sharing concerns early can prevent small problems from becoming larger issues. A successful development team depends on trust, accountability, and a shared understanding of what needs to be accomplished.
